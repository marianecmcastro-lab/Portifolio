Offer Creator: from single offers to creation at scale

How we reshaped the BEES promotions tool to handle a large volume of offers, with batch creation, autonomy for partners and components shared across the whole BEES One ecosystem.

RoleSenior Product Design Manager
CompanyBEES
Period2025
ScopeOffer and promotion creation and management

Context

Offer Creator is the BEES promotions management tool.

The volume of promotions was large, but the tool only allowed creating one offer at a time. Every promotion was set up from scratch, individually.

Challenge

Handle the volume without multiplying the effort of the people operating it, and get the tool ready to be used by partners themselves.

Project targets
  • Less time on task
  • Autonomy
  • Automation

My role

I led the project's design end to end. In total, 3 designers worked on it at different times: 2 senior and 1 mid-level.

Team

Design working together with product, architecture and the users themselves.

Design
Me and 3 designers at different times (2 senior and 1 mid-level)
Product
Constant alignment on defining the business rules
Architecture
Direct contact to ensure structural support for the tool
Users
Usability tests that shaped a linear, direct experience

What we proposed

Three complementary fronts: create faster, open the tool to partners, and connect everything to the same ecosystem.

  1. 01

    Batch creation (multi-creation)

    Instead of one offer at a time, users set up several offers in the same table, with date range, audience, products and conditions, and review everything before publishing. Less time per task.

  2. 02

    Self-service for partners

    The tool was adapted so partners can create and manage their own promotions, with autonomy and without depending on the BEES team.

  3. 03

    Shared components (enablers)

    Audience builder, to define the audience, and SKU selector, to choose products, became reusable components with their own management and governance.

Key decision

A single source of truth for the whole ecosystem.

Instead of each tool having its own way of choosing audiences and products, these building blocks became shared components, adaptable to different tools.

This way, every BEES One tool operates on the same source of truth: an offer's audience and products are the same anywhere in the ecosystem.

Impact

  • Partner adoptionA pilot with L'Oréal, using the tool as self-service.
  • Ready to scaleThe tool became ready to be rolled out to other partners.

Learnings

Naming the right concept defines the right solution. Identifying the concepts shows which solution actually streamlines the users' process. Here, the key was understanding the difference between bulk creation and multi-creation: in multi-creation, each offer is a different instance, and the way it is executed is different.

Testing with users matters more than the pattern. The previous structure was split into steps. Splitting a task into steps is a standard interface pattern when it is complex, but testing made it clear that it did not make sense for these users. The result was a linear, direct experience.
