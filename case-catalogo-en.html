Catalog management: from obstacle to reliable foundation

How we standardized the way partner catalogs enter BEES’s new product, variant and item structure, so the catalog would support the operation again instead of getting in its way.

RoleSenior Product Design Manager
CompanyBEES
Period2026
ScopePartner catalog ingestion and management

Context

BEES implemented a new catalog structure based on three management tiers: product, variant and item. The item is the SKU, the unit that is actually sold.

At the same time, partners would start selling their own products in their stores on BEES. Their catalogs would have to fit this new structure.

Problem

Data ingestion was the main bottleneck. Each partner had its own process and its own way of organizing the catalog, and none of it matched the product, variant and item structure.

  • Duplicate SKUsThe same item registered more than once, under different codes.
  • Duplicate productsThe same product living in several unrelated records.
  • Variants as itemsVersions of the same product treated as standalone items, breaking the hierarchy.

The result was very complex management. Instead of helping the operation, the catalog was getting in its way.

My role

I led design end to end, with a senior designer dedicated to the project.

We aligned commercial expectations, technical debt, product structure and business rules. And we defined how importing would work in practice: how to catalog what comes from the ERP as a product, variant or item, and how to create a new product when it doesn’t exist in the catalog yet.

What we proposed

Instead of adapting the platform to each partner, we flipped the logic: a single way in and a single catalog standard, with BEES responsible for the quality of product information.

  1. 01

    ERP ingestion, in a single batch

    The partner’s catalog comes straight from their ERP in a single batch, replacing a different process for each partner.

  2. 02

    Algorithm-assisted cleanup

    An algorithm reads the ERP data and suggests whether each record is a product, variant or item. The taxonomy manager confirms the suggestion or corrects it when needed, before the catalog reaches the store.

  3. 03

    BEES owns product information

    BEES manages product information and delivers a ready, consistent catalog to run in the e-commerce app.

The experience

Two screens sum up the flow: the overview of items coming from the ERP, and the item-by-item review done by the taxonomy manager.

Key decision

Automation should follow the algorithm’s maturity.

My initial proposal was to work by exception: only what the algorithm classified with low confidence would go through human review.

But a decision like that depends on how reliable the algorithm already is, and it was still maturing. So we chose sequential management of new items coming from the ERP, or individual review, with the taxonomy manager selecting which items to catalog.

Technical constraint

The first ERP ingestion has to be a full load, not incremental. The experience was designed around that first large volume.

Impact

Partner catalogs no longer have duplicates. With the algorithm suggesting how to classify each record, ERP data now lands at the right product, variant or item level.

Consistency didn’t stay in the catalog: it reaches every tool that depends on product information.

Tools now receiving consistent data
  • Inventory
  • Product selection
  • Offer creation
  • Delivery

Learnings

Change alongside the operation. In e-commerce, the catalog can’t be paused to be updated: partners keep selling. New structures and solutions have to be designed to run in parallel.

Trusting automation is a product decision. How much to delegate to an algorithm depends on its real maturity, and design has to keep pace with that evolution.
