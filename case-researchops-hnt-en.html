Structuring the UX Research practice at Hortifruti Natural da Terra

How user research became part of the product process of a company going through digital transformation: from the first survey to a custom Atomic Research model, with hypotheses that feed the next experiment.

RoleUX, Research and ResearchOps
CompanyHortifruti Natural da Terra (HNT)
Period2021 – 2022
ScopeUX Research and ResearchOps playbook

Context

HNT entered e-commerce in 2019, building on its authority in fresh produce. It started with a partnership and marketplace model, moved to white label and, in 2021, began building its own platform.

During the pandemic, delivery grew fast, first through WhatsApp and forms and later through a mobile-first ordering platform. The digital leadership was expanding the product area and agile culture, and UX was arriving as a core part of building new products.

Problem

The company was taking its first steps in user experience research. Product and Business Intelligence already ran data and market studies with valuable findings, but there was no structure to share them with the team.

The company also lacked a recruitment process, LGPD (Brazilian data protection law) compliance and defined research tools.

My role

I developed the company's UX Research and ResearchOps playbook: research plan, CSD matrix, Value Proposition Canvas, surveys, service blueprint, product mapping, product discovery and workshops. I also designed the DesignOps overview for the UX area, in January 2022.

Approach

The UX area was designed on an agile way of working, with three pillars: Discovery and Conceptualization, Product Design and Development, from handoff through quality and usability testing.

For research, I used as a reference the three-pillar structure described by Dani Matos on ResearchOps at QuintoAndar, since it fit the company's maturity.

Key decision

Given the company's maturity, instead of building a team of researchers I bet on generalist designers and research workshops, so research would become part of everyone's work.

Solution

Consolidating the practice Stakeholder workshops, a UX Wiki, case presentations and empirical tests, to make research a step of the product process.
Research infrastructure Templates, internal allies (BI, CRM, Customer Service, Legal and Product) and a defined recruitment and rewards process.
Learning and growth Research training workshops and a custom Atomic Research model.

UX Wiki. Documentation of processes, templates and frameworks, shared with the whole team. It started in Confluence, which was already part of the company's Office 365 package.

Empirical test. A survey in the discovery phase of the Encomenda (pre-order) product, to understand the buying behavior of omnichannel customers during the year-end holidays. Above: the three-week research plan and the Value Proposition Canvas built for the Christmas dinner pre-order.

Templates. Standardized research requests, based on Sheylla Lima's research brief template, which suited the company's stage.

Atomic Research, with hypotheses. I adapted Daniel Pidcock's concept and added a fixed hypotheses block: every study at HNT must generate new hypotheses to validate in new experiments, keeping learning constant. Above, the original Atomic Research model. Source: Daniel Pidcock.

Planned next steps

Research consent form reviewed by Legal, with LGPD compliance. Templates for research and usability test tabulation, and a recruitment screener. Analysis of the customer base that already consented to take part in research, plus budget and rewards definition. Atomization of existing market, buying profile and sentiment studies.

Impact

The company gained a structure to confirm or refute hypotheses that, until then, came only from the operations and executive perspective. The real user perspective became part of the process as a decision-making tool, to evolve, scale and raise the excellence of its products.

Learnings

New processes only take hold with buy-in: getting the people involved on board matters as much as the method itself. And low maturity in a growing business is not a blocker, as long as the value of the outcome is made evident to those who decide.
