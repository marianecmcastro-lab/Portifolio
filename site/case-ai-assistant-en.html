AI assistant for the design team

I conceived and orchestrated a conversational AI partner that supports product designers, from investigating the problem to defining next steps. It doesn't replace research, UX writing, design system teams or product decisions: it exposes assumptions, surfaces risks and structures evidence, keeping decisions with the people responsible for them.

RoleSenior Product Design Manager
CompanyBEES
Period2026
ScopeAI applied to the design process

What I can share

General context

Design teams in large-scale operations deal with complex initiatives, dense business rules and little time to structure their thinking. What was missing was support that helps people think better, not just produce faster.

My role

I led the conception and construction of the orchestration: the design of the agents and workflows, how they are triggered, and how they adapt to the non-linear way the team works. I started from an open orchestration methodology and adapted it to the team's needs.

How it was designed

Helping people understand problems, challenge assumptions and decide with more information, without deciding for them.

Two ways of working

A guided process, from discovery to a high-fidelity navigable prototype, for initiatives that are already structured. And a free conversation, to explore ideas and opportunities that have not become projects yet.

Principles
AI recommends, people decideEvery decision must be made explicitly by the person responsible for it.
Evidence before fictionMaterial claims require a traceable source. A recommendation is never presented as a confirmed decision.
Questions before analysisBefore analyzing, it asks, to understand what the person really needs.
Nothing is saved without permissionThe person authorizes what is saved and the scope of it.
Lesson learned

Very long conversations degrade the quality of AI, because it tends to lose what came earlier. The answer was to structure the work in stages that record what was learned, instead of relying on a single conversation.
