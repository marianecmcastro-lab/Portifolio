Billing: from spreadsheets to partner-approved charges

How we turned a manual invoicing process into a tool where every charge is validated by the partner before it becomes an invoice, handling highly customized contracts without multiplying errors and costs.

RoleSenior Product Design Manager
CompanyBEES
Period2025
ScopeProformas, invoices and charge approval

Context

BEES handled all partner invoicing manually, with Excel spreadsheets and other manual controls. A dedicated tool was key to streamlining the process.

The idea: each account’s account manager creates proformas and invoices, and the partner approves the billing data.

Challenge

Contracts are highly customizable. Each partner has its own combination of terms, and nothing repeats from one contract to the next.

  • Discounts
  • Products
  • Services
  • Charge types

And mistakes are expensive: an invoice with wrong data has to be cancelled and reissued, which raises the production cost of each charge.

My role

I provided end-to-end strategic leadership, managing 2 designers and partnering with product, architecture and engineering.

Team

Design, product, architecture and engineering working together from the start.

Design leadership
Me, managing 2 designers
Product
A product manager (PM) and a group product manager (GPM)
Architecture
A solutions architect
Engineering
An engineering manager

Key decision

Validate the charge before issuing the invoice.

Instead of generating the invoice directly, the flow starts with a proforma that the partner approves. Only after approval is the invoice created. Errors get fixed while they are still cheap, before there is a fiscal document to cancel.

A two-phase approach

We split the tool into two parts: first, make sure no charge went out without validation; then, reduce the manual work of building each proforma. The first phase shipped; the second is the next step.

  1. 01

    Approval structure

    The proforma, approval and invoice flow, connected to approval and notification components: the partner knows right away that a proforma is waiting and can approve it quickly.

    On the BEES side, the finance analyst gets a complete view of every charge:

    • all charges in one place
    • management of each charge
    • notifications for when to generate the invoice
    • approval tracking
  2. 02

    Connecting the service catalog

    Next step

    The second phase connects proforma and invoice generation to a service catalog: the catalog of services and products BEES sells to its partners.

    Contract customization is still there, but each contract’s base data would arrive pre-filled, making it easier for the analyst to build the proforma and the invoice.

Next step: AI

An AI agent for the part that is still manual: customization.

As a next step, I proposed connecting the service catalog to the base of contracts and negotiations documented by the commercial team, with the help of an AI agent. It would bring the customized data pre-filled, and the analyst would approve it before the proforma and invoice are generated.

The proposal was presented and has not been executed yet. The planned sequence: first the service catalog, then AI automation.

What it would bring
  • Lower operational cost
  • Less time on task
  • More autonomy for the analyst
  • A first step toward automating the service

Same logic as the tool itself: AI prepares, people validate. The decision stays with the analyst.

Impact

  • Faster approvalPartners now approve proformas faster.
  • Fewer errorsWith validation before issuing, errors and cancelled invoices went down.
  • A single sourceNo more spreadsheets scattered across different places, which could cause financial misalignment.

Learnings

Scalability from day one. The goal was never to move the manual process into a tool, but to build solid foundations so it can be automated, cutting costs and giving the company room to scale.

That is why the phases come in this order: first validate every charge, then structure the service catalog and, finally, automate with AI.
